Executive Summary
  • Payrolls turned negative: nonfarm employment fell by 23,000, well below the +34,000/month average of the past 12 months. May and June were also revised down by a combined 103,000.
  • Unemployment fell, but for mixed reasons: the rate eased to 4.1% (from 4.2%), driven by declines in teen (12.1%) and Hispanic (4.6%) unemployment; at the same time, temporary layoffs rose by 153,000, to 921,000.
  • Losses were concentrated: local government education (-50K) and retail trade (-19K) drove the weakness; health care (+22K) kept trending up, but at a slower pace than its 12-month average (+36K).
  • No inflationary wage signal: average hourly earnings rose just 2 cents ($37.62), +3.2% year-over-year, with no acceleration.
  • Watch the calendar: the preliminary annual benchmark revision is due August 28 and could meaningfully alter the recent payroll trend narrative.

Executive Summary
  • Payrolls roughly on trend: nonfarm employment rose by 57,000, close to the +36,000/month average of the past 12 months. April and May were revised down by a combined 74,000.
  • Unemployment eased slightly: the rate fell to 4.2% (from 4.3%), with 7.1 million people unemployed; participation dropped 0.3pp to 61.5% and the employment-population ratio slipped to 59.0%.
  • Growth concentrated in services: professional and business services (+36K), social assistance (+25K) and health care (+22K) kept trending up; leisure and hospitality lost 61,000 jobs on weak seasonal hiring.
  • Wages steady: average hourly earnings rose 13 cents to $37.64 (+3.5% year-over-year), with no sign of acceleration.
  • Watch the calendar: the July 2026 Employment Situation report is due August 7, and the preliminary annual benchmark revision is due August 28, 2026.
